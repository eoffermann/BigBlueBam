# Marketing positioning brief — 2026-06-18 site rebuild

Input for the `new-marketing-0618` site rewrite. Built per `docs/marketing-voice.md`
("Lead with the problem" + "The competition"). The site copy leads with these
pains and answers them; it does **not** name the rivals. Stats below are real and
citable — use them; don't invent more.

## The five problems we lead with (and who feels them)

1. **Tool sprawl & context-switching.** The average company now runs **100+ SaaS
   apps** (Okta 2025: 101; enterprises 275–291 per Zylo/JumpCloud). Workers spend
   **up to 9% of the day** just switching between them — ~half a workday a week —
   and **1 in 5 lose 2+ hours/week** to tool fatigue, ~100 hours (2.5 workweeks) a
   year (Lokalise/Cornell 2025). **79% of companies have done nothing about it.**
   → *Our answer:* sixteen apps that behave like one product. A Bond deal becomes a
   Bam project; a Board sticky becomes a task; a won deal becomes a Bill invoice —
   no tab-switching, no re-keying, no "which tool was that in."

2. **The AI bolt-on (and the AI upcharge).** **73% of SaaS vendors now charge
   extra for AI**; **68% lock it behind premium tiers** (Thunderbit 2026), and most
   "AI" is a chat sidebar pasted onto last year's product — it summarizes the doc
   you just wrote. The market is moving to AI-*native*, "built around AI, not
   enhanced by it."
   → *Our answer:* every app is driven by real MCP tools (720+) an agent operates
   at full parity with a person — same boards, same permissions, same audit trail.
   Not a sidebar. Not an upcharge. It's the architecture.

3. **Per-seat pricing in the agent era.** Per-seat is collapsing (adoption 21%→15%
   in a year; per-seat-heavy vendors churn 2.3× faster — MindStudio/SaaS CFO 2025):
   you pay per human, but agents do the work — so do you buy a seat for every bot?
   Salesforce, Microsoft, Google, and Atlassian *all* raised prices again in 2025;
   60% of vendors mask the increases.
   → *Our answer:* open source and self-hosted. Run it on your hardware. Add agents
   without a per-seat tax. No surprise renewal letter.

4. **Vendor lock-in / data hostage.** Proprietary formats, restricted or fee-based
   export, "contact sales to export your own data" (SmartSaaS 2025).
   → *Our answer:* open source. Read it, run it, host it, export it. Your data and
   your stack are yours — Postgres you can query, an Apache-style license you can
   trust.

5. **"All-in-one means shallow."** The real buyer objection: suites spread too thin
   and miss the depth of specialists; the big ones also get slow at scale (Notion
   past ~10k records) and overwhelming to set up (ClickUp's learning curve).
   → *Our answer:* modern architecture. Built recently as one coherent system — not
   a decade of acquisitions stitched together with webhooks — so each app is
   genuinely deep *and* they're one product. Fast because it's whole, not despite it.

## The four pillars (why we win — only claim these)

- **Modern architecture** — one stack, one data model, built as a single system.
- **AI-first, for real** — MCP parity, not a bolt-on; agents are teammates.
- **Open source** — readable, self-hostable, exportable, no hostage data.
- **The blended human/AI workplace** — the single strongest contender for the way
  tomorrow's business actually runs: people and agents on the same surfaces.

## Per-surface "lead with this pain"

- **Home / hero:** tool sprawl + the agent era. The day lost to tab-switching, and
  the new hires (AI) that never ask where anything lives.
- **AI section:** the bolt-on/upcharge. "Most 'AI' is a chatbot in the corner that
  charges you extra to summarize what you just typed." Then: full MCP parity.
- **Architecture section:** "all-in-one means shallow" + lock-in. One system vs.
  stitched acquisitions; open source vs. data hostage.
- **/work:** the planning tab you forget to open; status updates re-keyed across
  three tools.
- **/communicate:** chat that's a black hole — decisions lost in scrollback, an
  "AI" that can't actually *do* anything in the thread.
- **/sales:** the CRM nobody updates; the deal that closes and then nothing
  downstream happens automatically.
- **/operations:** invoices, forms, scheduling, analytics living in four more
  subscriptions that don't know your projects exist.
- **Per app:** open on that category's signature gripe (CRM = data entry nobody
  does; helpdesk = tickets disconnected from the work; whiteboard = ideas that die
  on the canvas; etc.), then the product as the way out, alongside the suite.
